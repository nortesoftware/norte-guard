WHAT THE RECORD ITSELF GOT WRONG
  394 packuments declare a `created` LATER than their own earliest release.
  npm resets it when it republishes a removed name as 0.0.1-security, so a
  package that lived five days reads as newborn — and `young` is one of the
  three conjuncts the quarantine filter selects on. Every age in this run is
  taken from the release timestamps instead.
  385 captures carry that defect and are NOT in the class once the age is repaired.

  1 publications were dropped because the account resolves to npm itself.
  0 captured versions ARE npm's 0.0.1-security placeholder. Attributing those to
  the registry would have grouped unrelated removals into one fabricated
  campaign — the OIDC-bot defect in a second costume, in the direction that
  manufactures a finding rather than the one that hides it.

WHAT THIS CANNOT SAY
  - The pool is this collector's capture filter, not npm. Every base rate
    here is a rate within an already-enriched population.
  - The family results are DESCRIPTIVE. With 4 malicious families there is no
    case arm to test against a control arm, and none is claimed.
  - `all first publications` is read at capture time. A name captured at its
    first release may have published again afterwards, and this run would not
    know. The error inflates the endpoint on both arms.
  - The hour of publication is recorded and NOT tested. A UTC hour is the
    publisher's longitude before it is anything else, and 8 case accounts
    is one draw from a timezone distribution.
  - Nothing here scores. No endpoint contributes a point to any verdict.
